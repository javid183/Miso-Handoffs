# Family Roles, round 3: creative critic

Critic: `miso-design-critic`, **creative lens** (feeling, fantasy, moments, originality, the family-under-pressure tone). Reviewed: the "Round 3" section of [`2026-11-09-family-roles-round2-design.md`](2026-11-09-family-roles-round2-design.md), against `vision.md`, the character sheets (`design/gdd/characters/`), the Demo Spec's Home affinities and Bond, and my round-2 notes. It also weighs THJ's two follow-ups of 2026-11-10 00:51 (John's limit should be stamina or posture, not Wrath, and damage-based, not "3") and 00:52 (Lucy is double-gated). Read-only; 2026-11-10.

## Strongest point: the family web runs one way, and Papa sits outside it

Draw R3's arrows. An enemy hits a child; **Mark** throws it; **Linda** shoots what the family moved; **Lucy** follows whoever is beside her. Three siblings, each set off by another's action: that's the combo feeling THJ asked for, and it's the best structure the roles have had. But **John is outside it**. No family member's action sets off his role (only enemies do). His payoff (Wrath, Blood Debt) is his own, on his own turn. And with a free swap at any range and no limit, nothing can ever happen *to* him. So the family's protector is untouchable and never needs his family. That's why he reads as both "too strong" and "passive": he's the one Bergson nobody plays *with*. In a game whose tone is a family under pressure, the strongest beat available is the reverse of the father's: **Papa can't hold any more, and the children cover him.** R3 already has the parts. Mark counters hits on John (John is "another family member"). Linda's protection clause fires on an enemy that ends next to John. Lucy beside John is Sheltered. All they need is a moment when John is down. **Change:** bring back a limit, THJ's way (point 1). It's damage-based endurance, not anger, and it's separate from his attack. When it breaks, John kneels and the three roles that already exist become the children answering for their father. That closes the web, and it gives the round its best-told moment, which S8 doesn't (point 2).

## Points

### 1. John: a posture that breaks under weight, not a count, not Wrath

THJ is right, and so was his correction of my round-2 note. "Wrath boils over, he throws his shield aside" (my round-2 point 3) is a warrior's fantasy. A father whose children are under attack doesn't strike; he holds. And a limit of 3 counts the wrong thing: three Claw scratches spend it, and a Goblin Queen strike costs the same as a scratch. R3 overcorrects by dropping the limit entirely. Then the price is only "where the child lands". That's a real tactical cost, but it isn't felt as a cost: nothing happens to John's body, and at ×0.5 × 0.6 he takes about 0.3 of each hit, anywhere on the map, without limit.

**Change: Posture** (working name; `miso-narrative` to name it: "Endurance", "Footing", "Steadfast").
- **What it measures:** the damage John takes **for others** (guarded hits only). A bar under his portrait starts each enemy turn full; every swap drains it by the damage he actually took. That keeps it readable ("it empties when he protects"). Hits on John himself don't drain it, so his front-line play stays his own.
- **Starting size (for systems to tune):** about 8 (a quarter of his 32 HP). That's about 4 guarded Claws at Early (≈2 each), about 6 with Shield Up (≈1.2 each), and about 1 guarded Queen strike. So it holds against small hits and breaks against a boss, which is the shape THJ asked for.
- **Shield Up needs no new rule:** its ×0.6 already makes Posture last longer, so "brace before the enemy turn" is a reason to raise it, never a gate. It's also the answer to "passive": on his own turn John chooses between bracing (lasting longer for the family) and acting.
- **When it breaks:** John drops to one knee; he can't swap until his next turn. That's all: no damage penalty, nothing on his attack. A child's bark ("Papa!") and the camera holding on him for a beat.
- **Separate from Wrath:** Wrath stays the built reward (JO-1), untouched by Posture. If THJ now dislikes the *name* Wrath for a father who punishes afterwards, rename it ("Resolve") with no mechanical change; that's narrative's call.
- **What it buys:** the bosses become the fights where Papa breaks (M4's Queen), the scenes a player retells. It also limits the any-range swap without a range limit or a per-turn cap, so Q2's levers (b) and ×0.7 aren't needed.
- **Keep:** any range, free, no gate. Stage the swap as John running or dashing across, never a teleport: a father crossing the room, which FR-13's tether can draw.

### 2. S8 is a Rube Goldberg machine until the player makes one choice inside it

As drawn, S8 is three presses with one right answer each (press, press, press), set off by an enemy's choice of target, on a board in an "assumed mid-fight state". The player authored "the sisters stand in a row" two turns ago, at best. Then they watch. Three ~1 s prompts in a row with no choice play as a rhythm game, not a family. Persona 5's Baton Pass works because the player hands the turn on; the all-out attack works because the player sets it up and then gets a splash. S8 has the splash without the hand-off.

**Change (two forks, either suffices; both are better):**
- **John is the fork.** Put John in S8 (on the same board, say at 0,0 within sight). His swap prompt comes first, at the start of the attack. *Pressed:* Papa takes it on Lucy's tile, Lucy lands on his. Mark still counters (John is family) and throws it east off John, and Linda still fires. Lucy is now away from Linda, so the chain stops at two: **"Papa took it, and his children answered."** *Declined:* the full three-beat chain, and the little sister lands the kill on the Claw that hit her. That choice (shield her, or trust the others to punish it) is the over-the-bar moment, and both answers are reasonable.
- **Mark's throw is a pick** (point 3), so beat 2 is authored live: the player sends the Claw into Linda's row on purpose.
- **Stage the payoff once:** when three roles chain from one hit, a short family cut-in (the four portraits, P5's all-out attack is the reference). And when the victim lands the last blow, give her the line. Keep it rare by construction; it shouldn't need a cooldown.
- **Check the S5 cousin:** in S5 Linda's Watch from 2,1 to 2,5 passes Mark on 2,3, 2 cells from her. With Catch & Return bought (MA-3: an arrow reaching him from 2+ cells along its line), **a Catch prompt fires and, if pressed, the Watch never reaches the Vaulted enemy.** That's a fourth prompt in the combo, and it cancels the combo. That's the exact tipping point into Rube Goldberg. Rule it out: Catch ignores Watch arrows fired on an enemy the family just moved, or Catch only fires on Linda's own turn.

### 3. Mark: the Setter, but he *throws*, and he catches what flies

**Per option:**

| Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Setter as written | Ship with a change | Right verb ("get off her") and the right family job, but Mark becomes plumbing: the moment belongs to Linda and Lucy, and his own decision shrinks to "be within 2" |
| Tag-in | Don't | A third follow-up (Continuous Attack and Lucy already follow); a kill chain is genre-default and puts nobody in danger |
| Shared Calm | Not as a role | The truest to the monk, but no combo and no moment beyond a pip drop. It belongs to Lucy's Mark gift instead (point 5) |
| The Call | Don't | "Lucy, left!" is a lovely bark, but it's John's job (protection before the hit) done smaller |

**The coordinator's flag:** pick **"away from the victim"** as the default reading. It's what "knocks it off them" means, and it reads from the board. Then fix the decision row honestly: Mark's own choice is the pick below, not his side.

**Change: the Setter throws (Command on the enemy turn).** *"When an enemy hits a family member, Mark strikes it and throws it off them, and the player picks where it lands."* The candidates are the free tiles beside the attacker that aren't closer to the victim, 1 tile (2 with Command unlocked, if systems allow). The prompt pauses (`requiredTimeScale` 0, as for Linda's and Lucy's), with "away from the victim" pre-selected so a quick press still works.
- **Why it's exciting:** Mark gets the moment, a live decision on the enemy's turn ("I threw it into Linda's row"), and it's his signature verb, Command, at reflex speed. The monk who directs the family's fight.
- **Division of labour, worth saying to THJ in one line:** *John moves the family; Mark moves the enemy; Linda shoots what moves; Lucy follows who's near.*

**Add the ranged half (the new idea THJ asked for):** an attacker the Setter can't reach (an Archer's arrow at a sibling) is **caught**. Mark, within 2 of the sibling, catches the arrow and returns it along its line. The tech exists: `ProjectileCatchAndReturn` (MA-3) already catches projectiles; this points it at enemy arrows. A barefoot monk plucking an arrow out of the air before it reaches his little sister is the single most "tell a friend" image any Mark option has had, and it's true to Atman's Focus. Keep it one role in one sentence: *"Mark answers whoever hurts the family: a blow, he throws its owner off them; an arrow, he catches it."* (If one clause is too many, the catch can be a Mark skill-tree node on top of the Setter.)

**What would change my mind:** if the throw pick slows the enemy turn enough that THJ turns the pause off, fall back to the Setter as written (away from the victim).

### 4. Lucy: one gate, and it's "beside someone"

THJ is right that "beside her" plus "within 3 of her" is two gates. **Keep "beside" and drop the reach.** "Beside" is the story: the youngest close to someone. The reach is bookkeeping, and it's out of character: her embers *chase* (her sheet's own word). An ember following her sister's arrow across the board is the image. A 3-tile limit makes it fizzle at exactly the moment the combo is most visible.
- **The worry:** Lucy tucked beside Linda at the back follows every arrow from safety. In fiction that's the little sister behind her big sister, and the price is real: two units clustered, and Linda's lines and Lucy's 15 HP sharing one flank, so one Claw threatens both.
- **If systems shows it's too strong,** limit it *per striker* (once per sibling per turn), never by distance. Distance is the gate THJ just removed.
- **Keep the edge rule** "beside her when they acted" (the Dash away still counts: "they launch together").

### 5. Lucy's gifts tell the better story, so earn them at Home, and keep Papa's Shelter Papa's

Family Shelter ("beside anyone, +1 ember level") is clean, but it erases the best-named thing in her kit: **Papa's** Shelter. "Why does only John reward her?" has a true answer: because he's her father, and that's what shelter means. Making Linda and Mark shelter her the same way flattens three relationships into one stat.

**Change:** keep Papa's Shelter as built. Make the sibling gifts **Bond rewards at Home** (Lucy–Linda and Lucy–Mark Bond level 2, where Bond exists, `thj/home-test`), so each one arrives with its Bond scene. That way the complexity is learned one rule at a time over a campaign, not three on day one, and it ties the role to the fourth USP ("Managing the Home as a Family").
- **Linda's gift:** with the reach dropped, "reach 4" has no job. Make it *"her ember rides her sister's arrow"*: the follow-up hits the arrow's target and the next enemy along its line.
- **Mark's gift:** Shared Calm, moved here: *"Beside Mark, Lucy shares his barrier"* (Atman's Focus covering the little sister). It fits his Yard Lesson (Focus) and his narrative of rejoining the family.
- **Drop the Mark gift "her follow-up knocks back":** it copies the Setter.

### 6. Linda: right as written. Name what she is

Cause ("the family moved it") and protection ("it came for the family") is the right pair. Keep both; cause-only would leave her silent on most enemy turns. The creative gap is that nobody can say her role in a word. John shields, Mark throws, Lucy follows; Linda **finishes**. Say it in her tooltip and barks: she's the one who closes what the family opens.
- **Watch for the S8 image:** the arrow passing over Lucy's head is a good picture if staged (Lucy ducks), and a bug if it reads as a near-miss on an ally. Check it with Catch (point 2).
- **Toba counts as family** for the cause clause, as recommended: he fights beside them.

## Per option (round 3)

- **A (as built):** fallback only.
- **P2:** superseded; its gate and the count-break are rightly gone.
- **R3:** ship with changes. John's Posture (point 1, THJ's own direction), John in S8 as the fork (point 2), Mark's throw plus catch (point 3), Lucy with "beside" only (point 4), Papa's Shelter kept and the gifts earned through Bond (point 5).

## What would change my mind

- **On Posture:** if, in the session or after chunk 2, John's free unlimited swap already produces declines (S6's prompts 2 and 3) often enough that players describe him as *choosing* whom to save. Then position is a felt price, and Posture can wait for the bosses.
- **On S8:** if a cold player, asked "did you feel a combo form?", describes the three-beat chain as something *they* did ("I kept the girls in a row"). Then authorship from the family's turn is enough, and the forks are polish.
- **On Lucy without reach:** if, on the Ridge and the River, Lucy beside Linda follows more than half of Linda's shots from the back line. Then add the per-striker limit, not a distance.
